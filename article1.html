GARCH-GNN Hybrid Volatility Forecasting Framework: Architecture Design and Out-of-Sample Backtesting

Published: 2026 | Research: Financial Econometrics, Volatility Modeling, Graph Neural Networks

Abstract

Traditional GARCH-type models rely on linear lag structures to capture conditional heteroskedasticity and demonstrate reliable performance under stationary market regimes. However, they struggle with abrupt market jumps, structural breaks, and nonlinear cross-asset contagion effects. This work proposes a hybrid GARCH-GNN framework that combines classical econometric volatility dynamics with graph-based nonlinear feature learning. The model integrates linear heteroskedasticity memory from GARCH specifications and high-order topological correlation extracted by graph neural networks, establishing a balanced paradigm between interpretability and predictive capacity.

1. Introduction

Financial time series are widely characterized by volatility clustering, heavy tails, leverage effects and non-stationarity. GARCH family models have long become the standard tool in volatility econometrics due to their solid statistical foundation. Nevertheless, their inherent linear assumptions limit performance during systemic stress, cross-sector risk transmission and tail events. Deep learning methods excel at nonlinear fitting but often suffer from poor interpretability and unstable out-of-sample generalization.

2. Model Architecture

The proposed framework consists of two core components: a baseline GARCH variance generator and a graph neural network feature extractor. The GARCH module captures univariate volatility persistence, while the GNN module encodes cross-asset dependency graphs constructed from rolling-window correlation matrices.

  • Univariate GARCH(p,q) conditional variance recursion
  • High-frequency feature engineering: returns, realized volatility, skewness and spread indicators
  • Dynamic graph construction based on time-varying correlation
  • Graph convolution layers for cross-asset risk embedding
  • Final volatility fusion through weighted linear aggregation

3. Empirical Findings

Out-of-sample evaluations across global equity indices show that the GARCH-GNN hybrid model outperforms classic GARCH, EGARCH and TGARCH benchmarks under MSE, MAE and QLIKE loss functions. Significant improvements are observed during market turbulence periods, confirming its robustness for tail volatility calibration.

4. Conclusion

The integration of econometric modeling and graph deep learning provides a feasible path to reconcile statistical interpretability and nonlinear predictive power. Further research will extend this framework to multivariate stochastic volatility and high-dimensional global asset systems.
